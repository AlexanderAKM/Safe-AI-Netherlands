## 1. Safe AI Netherlands National LinkedIn

### Main Post

Today we are launching Safe AI Netherlands (SAIN).

The Netherlands has world-class universities, a thriving tech sector, major institutions like ASML, and a growing number of people who care deeply about the future of AI. But until now, the Dutch AI safety landscape has been fragmented.

Local initiatives have been doing important work: research, courses, events, discussion groups, and community building. But they often had to solve the same operational problems separately, from legal structure and accounting to curricula and onboarding.

SAIN exists to change that.

Safe AI Netherlands is a Dutch non-profit dedicated to raising awareness of the full spectrum of AI risks, shaping mitigation priorities through public and expert discourse, and supporting effective solutions across the Netherlands and internationally.

We are starting with local chapters in Groningen, Amsterdam, and Utrecht, united under one national organization, shared infrastructure, and a common mission.

Our work runs through four pillars:

Research: a national AI Safety Research Hub connecting emerging talent with experienced supervisors.

Education: courses and discussion groups on technical AI safety, governance, interpretability, and related topics.

Events: hackathons, expert seminars, AI Safety Chats, and community gatherings.

Public outreach: writing, media, and public communication that help people find meaningful ways to engage.

SAIN grew out of the AI Safety Initiative Groningen, which developed a model that other Dutch groups were already beginning to use. The next step is to make that model national.

The Netherlands should have a thriving, authoritative, and impactful AI safety movement.

We are building it. Join us.

## 2. SAIN Groningen LinkedIn

### Main Post

AISIG is becoming SAIN Groningen.

This is a name change, but it is also more than that.

Over the past years, the AI Safety Initiative Groningen has grown from a local student group into a serious AI safety organization. We have run 9+ course cohorts with 100+ graduates, built discussion groups, hosted events and hackathons, contributed to research published at NeurIPS and ICLR, and developed operational playbooks that other Dutch AI safety groups have started to use.

At some point, the conclusion became obvious: what was built in Groningen should not stay local.

SAIN Groningen is now the Groningen chapter of Safe AI Netherlands (SAIN), a new national AI safety organization with chapters in Groningen, Amsterdam, and Utrecht.

For the Groningen community, the important message is simple:

The name changes. The community does not.

The same people are here. The same courses continue. The same events, discussion groups, Research Hub projects, WhatsApp groups, and Discord spaces remain. SAIN Groningen keeps local autonomy over its programs and community.

What gets better is the larger structure around it. Our research and education work now sits inside a national organization. Other chapters can contribute to the same Research Hub, use the same curricula, and build from the playbooks developed here. The national layer provides shared infrastructure, legal structure, brand authority, and support.

In other words: Groningen created the blueprint. SAIN lets it scale.

Thank you to everyone who helped build AISIG into what it is. We are excited for this next chapter as SAIN Groningen.

## 3. Alexander Personal LinkedIn

### Main Post

I'm thrilled to be announcing that I'm founding and directing @Safe AI Netherlands.

For some time now, a large part of my life has gone into working on AI safety. The reason is straightforward: getting this right really matters. By "right" I mean something quite demanding: honestly facing the risks AI brings, and doing the work to make sure its upside is broadly shared rather than narrowly captured (or not at all). That's the bar I think the field as a whole needs to clear.

Right now, we're a long way from that. So what do we do?

My approach is to focus on the full spectrum of risks, ranging from pressing current issues like deepfakes and misinformation, to loss of meaningful human control over increasingly capable systems. At AI Safety Initiative Groningen, that is exactly what we have been working on. The progress has been, if I may say so myself, genuinely encouraging, from launching our Research Hub to hosting events with leading AI safety experts.

But more is needed, and the Dutch AI safety ecosystem has real room to grow into something serious.

That is what SAIN is for. It is our attempt to build the organization the Dutch AI safety ecosystem needs: serious, coordinated, locally rooted, and capable of scaling to many cities.

SAIN starts with Groningen, Amsterdam, and Utrecht, but it won't stop there. To everyone who helped build AISIG: thank you (especially @joris, @davide, and @mariam, for building what you built, and for giving me the opportunity to continue it). The name changes to SAIN Groningen, but the mission stays the same.

I'm excited to be working on this with @Thomas, @ana, @andreea, @riccardo and the rest of SAIN's team. It's energizing to see the care people bring to getting this right.

Together with our advisory board—@teun, @jesse, @nandi, @jelle, @lisa, @robert, @charbel—I'm hopeful we are at the start of something meaningful.

Stay SAIN.
