# **Introducing Safe AI Netherlands**

The Netherlands is home to world-class universities, a thriving tech sector, and major institutions like ASML. Yet it lacks a unified civil-society voice on AI safety. As AI systems reshape everything around us, the risks scale accordingly. This is a monumental transition, and we have the power to shape whether it is positive or negative, just or unjust.

But the Dutch AI safety landscape is fragmented. Local initiatives operate independently, duplicating effort, struggling with administrative overhead, and lacking the authority to influence institutions. Talented individuals who care about these issues often have no clear path from curiosity to meaningful contribution.

[**Safe AI Netherlands (SAIN)**](https://safeainetherlands.org/) exists to close that gap.

---

SAIN is a Dutch non-profit dedicated to raising awareness of the full spectrum of AI risks: from deepfakes, misinformation, and algorithmic manipulation to the loss of meaningful human control over increasingly capable systems, and everything in between. We contribute to shaping mitigation priorities through ongoing discourse and support the realization of effective solutions across the entire Netherlands (and internationally).

SAIN operates through local chapters: **SAIN Groningen**, **SAIN Amsterdam**, and **SAIN Utrecht**, united under a single brand, shared infrastructure, and a common mission. More chapters will follow.

Many of the things Dutch AI safety initiatives are doing (research, courses, events, community building) can be done far more effectively under a shared identity. And a lot of the operational and legal challenges local groups face, like registering a legal entity, setting up accounting, and developing curricula from scratch, can simply be removed by providing that infrastructure nationally. That's what SAIN enables.

## **What SAIN does**

SAIN's work runs through four pillars:

**Research.** We host a national [AI Safety Research Hub](https://safeainetherlands.org/research) that connects experienced supervisors (PhD+) with emerging talent to produce peer-reviewed research at top-tier global conferences. Our work has already been published at NeurIPS and ICLR, and we're just getting started.

**Education.** We deliver courses on AI safety, including our [AI Safety, Ethics, and Society](https://safeainetherlands.org/get-involved#:~:text=Courses,two%20tracks%20%E2%80%94%20Technical) course in both Technical and Governance tracks, alongside [discussion groups](https://safeainetherlands.org/get-involved#:~:text=Discussion%20Groups,to%20AI%20governance) on interpretability, AI governance, and more. Education is the primary entry point into the SAIN ecosystem. Over 100 people have graduated from these courses, and Amsterdam replicated the model with 70+ participants on their very first run.

**Events.** From technical hackathons and expert seminars to informal AI Safety Chats, our events attract newcomers and deepen the engagement of those already involved. We've spoken at TEDx, EAGx Amsterdam, AiGrunn, and Samenwerking Noord. We've consulted for a municipality on their AI governance framework.

**Public Relations.** Through our [Substack](https://safeainetherlands.substack.com), [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/safe-ai-netherlands/), and [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/sainetherlands/), we direct the public toward engagement entry points and amplify the work of all our chapters.

## **Why a national organization?**

Three reasons, in short.

**The whole is more than the sum of its parts.** Things like research, education, and events can be done far more effectively under a shared brand. SAIN's national Research Hub lets anyone from any chapter participate, and publications carry the weight of a national organization rather than a local group.

**Local groups shouldn't have to reinvent the wheel.** Starting a serious AI safety initiative in the Netherlands means registering a legal entity, setting up accounting, developing curricula from scratch, and figuring out operations through trial and error. With SAIN, a motivated group can adopt the brand, receive fiscal sponsorship through our legal entity, use ready-made curricula, follow documented playbooks, and get direct mentorship. Months of setup become weeks of community building.

**There is real untapped potential across the country.** Think of Eindhoven, an extremely valuable place to have a flourishing AI safety community because of the stream of highly competent students and the proximity to ASML. Or Amsterdam, with one of Europe's densest concentrations of AI talent. A national identity unlocks all of this.

## **A proven model**

SAIN didn't start from theory. It grew out of the [AI Safety Initiative Groningen (AISIG)](https://safeainetherlands.org/chapters/groningen), which in under a year transformed from a student group into a professionalized organization with a structure that other Dutch AI safety groups began adopting. When other cities are in need of the same infrastructure, the answer isn't to start one's own organization, but rather to build something national that everyone could thrive in.

Amsterdam replicated the course model and had 70+ participants on their very first independent run. The playbooks work. The 4-team operational model works. SAIN scales what has already been proven.

## **How it works**

SAIN is structured in such a way that local chapters can thrive. A strong national layer provides legal status, brand authority, financial infrastructure, and operational playbooks. Local chapters run their own courses, events, and community with autonomy over how they operate locally.

Every chapter benefits from the SAIN brand, the legal umbrella, access to the national Research Hub, standardized curricula, and mentorship from people who have already built a chapter. The logic is chapter-first: stronger chapters produce stronger outcomes. The national layer exists to enable that.

## **The SAIN funnel**

We provide a structured pathway from first contact to career-level contribution in AI safety:

1. **Initial engagement.** Attend an event, read a Substack post, see something on social media.  
2. **Foundational learning.** Complete a course, join a discussion group.  
3. **Active contribution.** Join a Research Hub project, participate in a campaign, join a chapter team.  
4. **Structured development.** Undertake a fellowship or internship in AI safety. We can help you get connected.  
5. **Professional impact.** Work full-time in AI safety: policy, research, industry, or civil society. We can help you get connected.

Every pillar, every chapter, and every team member plays a role in moving people along this path. Anyone interested can join.
